# Pricing Page (High‑Conversion)

Design a pricing page that helps visitors choose and feel good about it. Your job is not to “show prices.” Your job is to **reduce uncertainty**.

---

## 1. Before You Design/Write
Gather (ask if missing):

1.  **Offer + audience:** What are you selling? Who is it for? What’s the main value metric? (seat, usage, project, revenue, etc.)
2.  **Plans:** Plan names + prices (monthly/annual). Limits per plan (the 3–6 limits that matter). What’s the upgrade trigger?
3.  **Objections + risk:** Top 3 reasons people don’t buy today. Security/compliance needs? Can you offer: free trial, free plan, money-back, demo?
4.  **Proof:** Testimonials, logos, results, case studies, metrics.
5.  **Traffic context:** Are visitors coming from homepage, feature pages, ads? What do they already know?

---

## 2. Core Structure

### Above the Fold (Must Have)
*   **Clear value headline:** What outcome, for who.
*   **Monthly/Annual toggle:** With annual savings callout.
*   **Pricing Table:** 3‑plan pricing table (most common) or 2‑plan (simple product).
*   **Primary CTA per plan:** Consistent verbs.

### Below the Fold (High Leverage)
*   **Plan comparison:** Feature matrix or “what you get” bullets.
*   **FAQ:** Objection handling.
*   **Social proof:** Near decision points.
*   **Security/Compliance:** Procurement section (if B2B).
*   **Final CTA:** + contact sales.

---

## 3. Layout Types

*   **A) Classic 3‑card:** Best when you have 3 natural tiers (Starter / Pro / Business) and pricing is simple.
    *   *Rules:* 1 plan labeled "Recommended". Show “most popular” without yelling.
*   **B) Value metric slider:** Best when pricing scales with usage (seats, events, credits).
    *   *Rules:* Keep math obvious. Keep a safe default.
*   **C) “Pick your path” (Two columns):** Best when you have different audiences (Individuals vs Teams).
    *   *Rules:* Separate by persona first, then price.
*   **D) Enterprise last mile:** Best when you have a self-serve path + sales-led path.
    *   *Rules:* Enterprise should read like procurement reassurance.

---

## 4. High‑Conversion Strategies

1.  **Make the decision easy:** 3 plans max. One recommended plan. Bullets describe outcomes, not internal features.
2.  **Anchor value (without being shady):** Annual toggle with “Save X%”. Show “Starting at” only if pricing is truly variable. Avoid surprise fees.
3.  **Reduce risk:** Choose at least one: Free trial, Free plan, Money‑back guarantee, “Talk to sales” with a clear promise.
4.  **Handle objections before they bounce:** Add FAQs like "Can I cancel anytime?", "What happens if I hit limits?", "Security/data".
5.  **Provide a readable comparison:** Avoid huge spreadsheets. Group by: Core, Collaboration, Admin. Highlight what changes at each tier.

---

## 5. Copywriting Templates

**Headlines:**
*   “{Outcome} for {audience}—without {pain}”
*   “Plans that scale from {small} to {big}”

**Plan Description (2 lines):**
*   *Who it’s for & What it unlocks.* 
*   *Example:* Pro — For designers shipping weekly. Better components, faster iteration.

**CTA Buttons:**
*   Use verbs that match the motion: “Start free trial”, “Buy Pro”, “Contact sales”.
*   Keep CTAs consistent across plans.

**Feature Bullets (Outcomes):**
*   ❌ “Unlimited projects”
*   ✅ “Ship unlimited client sites without extra fees”

---

## 6. Pricing Table UI Checklist

- [ ] Visible monthly/annual toggle.
- [ ] “Save X%” callout on annual.
- [ ] Recommended plan styling (subtle highlight, e.g., borders/glow).
- [ ] Key limits visible (3–6 max).
- [ ] Included items visible (3–6 max).
- [ ] Clear next step under each plan.
- [ ] Link: “Compare plans” (scrolls to matrix).
- [ ] **Mobile:** Table becomes stacked cards (no horizontal scroll nightmare).

---

## 7. SEO + AEO Checklist

*   **Title:** “Pricing — {Product}” + outcome keyword.
*   **Meta description:** 1 sentence on value + 1 sentence on pricing starting point.
*   **Clean URL:** `/pricing`
*   **Internal links:** From homepage CTA, feature pages.
*   **AEO (Answer Engines):** Add FAQ section answering refund policy, trial length, cancellation, what counts as usage.
*   **Schema:** FAQ schema (if supported).

---

## 8. Common Pitfalls (Anti-Patterns)

- [ ] Too many plans (analysis paralysis).
- [ ] Features listed with no context (why it matters).
- [ ] Pricing hidden behind “Contact sales” for everything.
- [ ] Switching value metric mid-page (confusing).
- [ ] Over-designed table that harms readability.

---

## Output Format (For Prompts)
When requesting a pricing page generation:
1. Page outline (sections + order)
2. Pricing table spec (plans, bullets, limits, CTA)
3. FAQ list (6–12 Q/A)
4. SEO/AEO suggestions
5. Layout recommendation
